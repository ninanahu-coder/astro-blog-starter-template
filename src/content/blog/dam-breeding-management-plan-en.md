Healthy puppies begin with a healthy dam. This plan covers the complete workflow for the dam from **pre-breeding → mating → gestation → whelping room → lactation**, compiled from Australian regulations (RSPCA Australia, ANKC/Dogs Australia, NSW/Victoria breeding standards) and veterinary authorities (WSAVA, Merck Veterinary Manual, AKC, Royal Canin), and forming a closed loop with the *Improving Puppy Survival Rate Plan*.

> **Two important disclaimers**: (1) The clinical thresholds in this plan (temperature, progesterone, drug dosages) must be confirmed by a **supervising veterinarian** before use; (2) Australian breeding regulations **vary by state and change with amendments** — before formal implementation, verify the latest legislation in your state (some government pages are not directly accessible in this environment; figures are drawn from authoritative searches and must be confirmed against official sources).

## 1. Australian Regulations and Ethics (Comply Before Breeding) [AU]

- **RSPCA Australia**: supports mandatory registration of breeders; responsible breeders must — breed offspring free of known genetic disease, allow buyers to see the parent dogs, fully disclose medical history and screening results, and not compromise welfare for extreme appearance
- **ANKC / Dogs Australia**:
  - **Minimum age at mating for the dam is 12 months**, with an ideal breeding age of **2–6 years**
  - **Litter frequency: no more than 2 litters within 18 months** (unless approved by the member body)
  - **Dams over 8 years**: must have veterinary fitness certification from within 3 months prior to mating
- **NSW breeding standards** (legally enforceable under POCTAA): a breeding dam is defined as **1–8 years**; **a maximum of 5 litters in a lifetime** (or 3 caesarean sections, whichever comes first)
- **Victoria**: a maximum of 5 litters in a lifetime; reported maximum breeding age of 5 years (disputed; defer to current legislation); tiered registration by number of fertile dams (3–10 dams for the purpose of sale must be registered as a domestic animal business)
- **Microchipping**: all states require implantation before a puppy is sold/transferred, with the breeder's information recorded in the state registry
- **Note**: state law (NSW/Victoria maximum of 5 litters) is generally **stricter than ANKC** — apply the stricter of the two; WA/SA/ACT have additional rules, so check your own state

## 2. Pre-Breeding Health (Building the Dam's Foundation Before Pregnancy)

- **Vaccinations must be fully complete before pregnancy**: the dam should complete her core vaccines (Australian **C5**) before pregnancy, so that colostrum carries high antibody levels to protect the puppies. **Vaccination during pregnancy is prohibited** (especially live vaccines); antibody rise takes 30–45 days, so vaccinating in mid-to-late gestation is useless for this litter — **any due boosters should be completed 2–4 weeks before oestrus**
- **Deworming and parasite control**: before mating, ensure intestinal deworming and heartworm/flea/tick prevention are in place
- **Brucellosis testing** (Brucella canis): in endemic areas, test the dam before every mating and the stud dog every 6 months; infection causes late-gestation abortion. This disease is an emerging concern in Australia (imported dogs) and is recommended for inclusion in the pre-breeding examination
- **Body Condition Score (BCS)**: target **4–6 (on a 9-point scale, ideal 5)** at mating; too thin affects conception, too fat (7–9) reduces fertility and increases dystocia risk. Begin nutritional conditioning **2–3 months** before pregnancy
- **Breed health / genetic screening [AU]**:
  - **AVA/ANKC Canine Hip and Elbow Dysplasia Scheme (CHEDS)** — now administered by Dogs Australia; dams can be scored from ≥12 months
  - **ORCHID** health database + breed-specific DNA testing (via ANKC)
  - Add breed-specific tests as applicable: eyes (ACES), heart, breed genetic disease DNA (e.g. PRA, DM, etc.)

## 3. Oestrus and Mating (Precise Timing Using Progesterone)

- **Progesterone testing to pinpoint ovulation**: begin testing on **days 5–7** of oestrus (proestrus); progesterone rising above **4–8 ng/mL** indicates ovulation (most dams ovulate at around 5 ng/mL)
- **Optimal mating: 48–72 hours after ovulation** (eggs need a further 2–3 days to mature after release), i.e. approximately 4–7 days after the LH peak
- **Gestation: 63 ± 1 days from ovulation** (calculated from the mating date it fluctuates widely at 58–68 days, because sperm can survive several days) — **hormonal timing is far more accurate than counting from the mating date**

## 4. Gestation Management

- **Nutrition**: maintain normal maintenance food for the first 5–6 weeks; **switch to puppy (breeding) food at around week 6 / the final third of gestation** (70–80% of fetal growth occurs in the last 3–4 weeks). Energy requirements in the final third **increase by 25–50%** (up to 60% for large litters); as abdominal space is limited, switch to **small frequent meals or free feeding**. Protein ≥22–32%, energy ≥4 kcal/g dry matter
- **Never over-supplement calcium during pregnancy**: this downregulates calcium-regulating mechanisms and can trigger postpartum hypocalcaemia during lactation (see Section 6)
- **Gestational deworming (fenbendazole protocol)**: **50 mg/kg once daily, from gestation day 40 to postpartum day 14**, reducing transplacental/milk-borne transmission of roundworm by about 89–90% and hookworm by about 99% (follow veterinary advice)
- **Pregnancy checks**:
  - **Ultrasound: around day 25–30** — confirms pregnancy and fetal viability (not suitable for counting fetuses)
  - **X-ray: around day 55** — accurately **counts fetuses** (fetal skeletons only become visible on radiograph at days 45–50); fetal count is used to assess dystocia risk
- **Exercise**: maintain moderate normal exercise in early gestation; reduce vigorous activity in the final third, avoiding exhaustion and overheating

## 5. Whelping Room Preparation and Labour

- **Whelping box**: set up 1–2 weeks in advance in a quiet, warm, draught-free location so the dam can acclimatise; place a heating pad/lamp on one side of the puppy area to maintain **about 29–32°C** (leaving space for the dam to move away from the heat source)
- **Pre-labour temperature drop**: rectal temperature falls to about **37.1°C (36.7–37.8°C)**, i.e. **below about 37.2°C**, usually **8–24 hours** before whelping; begin measuring twice daily as the due date approaches
- **Stages of labour**:
  - First stage: restlessness, nesting, panting, shivering, reduced appetite, temperature drop; lasts 6–12 hours (up to 24)
  - Second stage: active straining to deliver puppies, **0–30 minutes per puppy is normal, with intervals of up to about 2 hours**
  - Third stage: expulsion of the placenta after each puppy (second and third stages alternate)

**Dystocia — contact a veterinarian immediately**:
- No delivery **24–36 hours** after the temperature drop
- **Strong contractions for more than 30 minutes** with no puppy delivered
- **More than 2 hours between two puppies** (especially when fetuses are known to remain)
- Active labour for more than 4 hours with no puppy
- **Green/black discharge without prompt delivery of a puppy** (a sign of placental separation, fetal distress)
- The dam is collapsed, weak, or a stuck puppy is visible
- **Caesarean considerations**: oversized fetus, uterine inertia, brachycephalic/large-headed breeds, malpresentation, fetal distress; precise ovulation timing + X-ray fetal count help schedule an elective caesarean

## 6. Postpartum Dam Care (Lactation)

- **Lactation nutrition**: energy requirements climb after whelping, **peaking at weeks 3–4 (–5)**; feed as a multiple of maintenance: week 1 at 1–1.5×, week 2 at 2×, weeks 3–4 at 2.5–3×, with a peak of up to **2–4×** for large litters. Continue puppy food, free feeding or frequent small meals. Peak calcium requirement is 2–5× maintenance, met through a balanced puppy food — **not through pre-emptive calcium supplementation during pregnancy**
- **Postpartum hypocalcaemia / eclampsia**: most common in **small breeds with large litters, at the lactation peak around 2–3 weeks postpartum**. Progression signs: reduced appetite → restlessness and panting → stiff, ataxic gait → muscle tremors, face-rubbing, drooling → tonic-clonic seizures, rising temperature → coma and death. A **medical emergency**; treatment is slow IV infusion of 10% calcium gluconate at 0.5–1.5 mL/kg (under cardiac monitoring, per veterinary direction); prevention relies on a balanced diet during lactation + no calcium supplementation during pregnancy
- **Mastitis**: mammary glands red, swollen, hot and painful, discoloured; milk abnormal/bloody/purulent; fever and lethargy — requires veterinary treatment and may require preventing puppies from nursing on the affected side
- **Metritis**: uterine infection from a few days to weeks postpartum — foul-smelling purulent discharge, fever, poor demeanour, poor mothering, reduced lactation, possibly with retained placenta/fetus — seek emergency care (systemic, potentially fatal)
- **Daily monitoring**: measure rectal temperature daily for the first week; appetite and water intake; vaginal discharge (normally green-brown → red, no foul odour, tapering off over about 3 weeks); check mammary glands to guard against mastitis; body condition; behaviour (quietly mothering vs neurological signs → postpartum hypocalcaemia)
