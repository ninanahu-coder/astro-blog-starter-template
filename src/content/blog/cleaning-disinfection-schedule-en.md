Effective cleaning comes down to **fixed timing, correct method, and adequate contact time**. This schedule slots cleaning and disinfection into specific time blocks that line up with the *Kennel Work Schedule*. The core principle never changes: **clean first (remove organic matter), then disinfect (kill pathogens) — and it only counts once the contact time is met.**

## Daily Cleaning & Disinfection Schedule

| Time | Task | Method | Disinfectant / contact time |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 07:00 – 07:45 | Morning dry clean-up | Scoop waste, remove leftover food and soiled bedding, sweep hair (dry before wet) | — |
| 07:45 – 08:30 | Bowls (AM) | Wash, soak in disinfectant, rinse and dry | QUAT, 10 min |
| 09:45 – 11:00 | Main kennel clean | Move dog out → scrub floor, wall base, gate with detergent → rinse | Routine QUAT 10 min, rinse and dry |
| 13:00 – 14:00 | Kennel disinfection (key) | Apply disinfectant to already-cleaned runs, room/zone by zone | Accelerated H₂O₂ 5–10 min |
| 15:15 – 16:00 | Bowls (PM) + yard clean | Wash/disinfect bowls; clear yard waste | QUAT 10 min |
| 17:00 – 17:45 | Drains / walkways | Flush drains, clear walkways, check for pooling water | Disinfect as needed, always dry |
| 18:00 – 18:15 | Handover round | Re-check each run for dryness, waste, and water level; fill in the handover sheet | — |

> Contact time is non-negotiable: rinsing off immediately = no disinfection. Use a timer. Bleach and some disinfectants must be rinsed off after to avoid burning paws.

> The time blocks above are aligned with the *Kennel Work Schedule*. If a vet round, group exercise, or a drop-off/pick-up peak intervenes, the whole schedule can be pushed back, but **the order must never be reversed**: always dry clean-up first, then detergent wash, then disinfect, and finally rinse and dry.

### Daily Handover Checklist (tick each item before leaving)

- [ ] Every run floor is dry, with no leftover faeces/urine and no pooling water
- [ ] Food and water bowls washed, disinfected, dried, and topped up (increase volume on hot days)
- [ ] Every zone has met its disinfectant contact time and been rinsed off
- [ ] Disinfectants were mixed fresh for use, with no leftover solution kept overnight
- [ ] Isolation-run tools are stored separately and not mixed with routine tools
- [ ] Handover sheet completed: abnormal dogs, unfinished tasks, disinfectant stock

## Disinfectant Quick Reference

| Disinfectant | Kills | Contact time | Key note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| QUAT (quaternary ammonium) | General bacteria, some viruses (**NOT parvovirus**) | ~10 min | Inactivated by organic matter/soap; routine daily use |
| Bleach (sodium hypochlorite) | Almost all pathogens including parvo | ~10 min | Parvo dilution **1:32** (~30 ml per litre); mix fresh, rinse and ventilate after |
| Accelerated hydrogen peroxide | Broad spectrum incl. parvo, kennel cough | ~5–10 min | Gentle, easy to rinse — **first choice for the puppy area** |
| Potassium peroxymonosulfate | Broad spectrum incl. parvo, tolerant of organic load | ~10 min | Common for deep cleaning / outbreaks |
| Chlorhexidine | General bacteria, some fungi (**NOT parvovirus**) | Per product instructions | Gentle, for routine mild cleaning in the puppy area; must not be used as a parvo disinfectant |

> Dilution ratio memo: for parvo, use bleach at **1:32** (~30 ml per litre); to kill parvo in the puppy area, use accelerated hydrogen peroxide at **1:16**. Always mix diluted solution fresh and use it up the same day; post the ratios on the cleaning-room wall — never estimate from memory.

## Isolation / Outbreak (additional)

| Trigger | Action | Disinfectant / contact time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vomiting, diarrhoea or coughing | Isolate immediately, dedicated tools, cleaned last by one person | Bleach 1:32 or peroxymonosulfate, 10 min |
| Kennel with parvo history | Thorough clean then enhanced disinfection; re-check for months | Bleach 1:32, 10 min, rinse and dry |
| Every contact with a different dog group | Wash/sanitise hands, change or disinfect footwear | Hand sanitiser |

## Perth Hot-Season Notes (summer, Dec–Feb)

Perth summers routinely exceed 35°C, so the cleaning and disinfection rhythm needs adjusting:

- **Do it while it's cool**: schedule the main kennel clean and disinfection for the cooler morning hours where possible, and avoid long stretches of work inside a stuffy, hot kennel at midday.
- **Floors dry faster — don't shorten the contact time**: in the heat, disinfectant evaporates quickly and the surface may "look dry" soon after spraying, but the contact time must still be counted in full; re-spray if needed to keep the surface wet until the timer is up.
- **Check water bowls more often**: dogs drink far more in the heat, so beyond the two scheduled bowl washes, check water levels in the afternoon and top up promptly to avoid empty bowls.
- **Damp and mould risk**: always dry thoroughly after rinsing — mould and bacteria multiply faster in warm, humid conditions; fans and cross-ventilation from open windows help drying.
- **Organic matter rots faster**: faeces and leftover food turn foul and attract flies faster in the heat, so morning dry clean-up and prompt scooping matter even more.
- **Chemical storage**: keep disinfectants out of direct sun and heat to avoid concentration loss and container deformation.

## Extra Precautions

1. **Never mix chemicals**: bleach + ammonia cleaner = toxic gas; bleach + QUAT cancel each other out. Use one at a time, rinse thoroughly before switching.
2. **Clean before you disinfect**: organic residue disables QUATs and bleach — the order can't be reversed.
3. **Always rinse + dry**: prevents paw burns and stops moisture breeding bacteria/mould.
4. **Don't pressure-hose contaminated waste**: it aerosolises pathogens — dry clean-up first.
5. **Zone-dedicated, colour-coded tools**: each site, the puppy area and isolation each use their own tools, never shared.
6. **Puppy area (K4) is strictest**: accelerated hydrogen peroxide, thorough rinsing, dedicated tools, ventilate before returning puppies.
7. **Chemical safety**: store separately, keep original labels, post dilutions on the wall, wear PPE (gloves, plus goggles/mask as needed).

## Common Mistakes (the easiest traps on the ground)

- ❌ **Rinsing right after spraying**: that's the same as not disinfecting. If the contact time isn't met, the pathogens aren't dead.
- ❌ **Spraying disinfectant before organic matter is washed off**: waste and soap residue inactivate QUAT and bleach — the clean-before-disinfect order can't be skipped.
- ❌ **Using QUAT for parvo**: QUAT does not kill parvovirus; parvo can only be handled with bleach at 1:32, accelerated hydrogen peroxide, or potassium peroxymonosulfate.
- ❌ **Borrowing tools across zones**: taking the isolation-zone mop over to the adult-dog zone — the tool itself becomes the vector.
- ❌ **Reusing diluted solution overnight**: bleach and the like must be mixed fresh; they lose potency once they sit.
- ❌ **Pressure-hosing contaminated waste**: aerosolisation blows pathogens all over the run — always dry clean-up first.
