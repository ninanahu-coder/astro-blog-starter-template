Core idea: **The dogs' daily routine is fixed; staff work and manager oversight are both designed around it, not the other way round.** One manager cannot keep track of every detail for 160+ dogs. The key to efficiency is management by exception: let the dogs that need attention surface automatically, don't watch the 90% that are normal, and focus energy on the 10% that are not.

## 1. Built Around "A Dog's Day"

| Time period | Dog's state and needs | Corresponding staff work | Manager's focus |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 07:00–09:00 Morning | Waking, eliminating, hungry; state of mind is easiest to read | Patrol, refill water, prepare meals | Compile morning-check yellows/reds, assign staff |
| 09:00–12:00 Late morning | Most energetic; needs exercise and elimination | Feed → clean kennels while dogs are out → first exercise round | Assign new admissions, cross-site scheduling |
| 12:00–13:00 Midday | Natural rest period | On-duty patrol, refill water | 12:00 phone round |
| 13:00–16:00 Afternoon | Second active period | Disinfect → second exercise round → dinner | Advance weekly tasks, supplies |
| 16:00–18:00 Evening | Calm after eating; pickup peak | K4 reception, evening patrol, close kennels | 17:45 daily report compilation |
| 18:00–07:00 Night | Sleep (puppies still need feeding and warmth) | K4 on-site staffing, patrol, remote monitoring | On-call duty, watch remote alarms |

## 2. Daily Staff Deployment and Dynamic Adjustment

Heavy work is concentrated in the two 1.5-person-hour periods when dogs are active (9–12, 13–16), which is exactly when cleaning and disinfection can be done while dogs are out of their kennels; on-duty periods handle only light work.

| Trigger situation | Adjustment action |
| --- | --- |
| An isolated sick dog appears at a site | Add staff, dedicate a person and tools, clean it last in the sequence |
| Increase in / newborn K4 puppies | Add night on-site staffing, assign a dedicated puppy handler |
| Weekend pickup peak | Add K4 front-desk staff, stagger appointments |
| Staff on leave | Nearest team provides flexible cover; manager covers the relief post |
| Extreme heat | Add water-refill patrols, check cooling |
| Veterinary rounds day | Stagger against deep disinfection, keep staff to accompany the vet |
| Suspected outbreak at a site | Seal and restrict the site, suspend cross-site staff movement, dedicate staff to the isolation area throughout |
| Power / cooling failure | Prioritize temperature control for the puppy and isolation areas, activate emergency contact and transfer plan |
| Large batch of new admissions (Friday / before long holidays) | Pre-stage health-screening staff, stagger admissions processing, fill the observation area first |

## 3. The Manager's Control System (Core)

### Per-Dog Three-Color Status

| Status | Meaning | Who handles it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 🟢 Green | Normal | Staff routine; manager does not intervene |
| 🟡 Yellow | Observation (mild abnormality) | Record + hand off, manager informed, recheck within 24h |
| 🔴 Red | Alert (abnormal / isolated / on medication) | Manager watches daily, vet if necessary |

**Each day the manager looks only at the yellow-and-red list**, seeing at a glance which dogs across the whole company need attention.

**Three-color grading criteria** (so front-line staff don't have to guess and grade consistently):

| Observation item | 🟢 Green | 🟡 Yellow (observe) | 🔴 Red (alert) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Eating | Finishes normally | Leaves one meal unfinished / picky | Refuses two meals in a row |
| Defecation | Well-formed, normal | Soft once | Diarrhea, blood, vomiting |
| State / activity | Lively, normal responses | Slightly subdued, tends to hide | Clearly listless, cannot stand |
| Respiratory | None | Occasional single cough / sneeze | Persistent cough, purulent nasal discharge, wheezing |
| Other | No abnormality | Mild limp / small wound | Heavy bleeding, seizures, bloat, unable to bear weight |

> Color-setting principle: **take the most severe item** — if any item falls to red, the whole dog is red. When unsure, **round up rather than down**, choosing one more round of observation. A yellow that does not recover or worsens within 24h automatically escalates to red.

### Manager's Daily Control Loop

| Time | Action | Content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 07:00–08:30 | Plan | Review last night's report and appointments, assign staff |
| 08:30 | Roll call | Roll call across four sites, compile yellows/reds |
| 12:00 | Monitor | Round check, confirm task completion and new abnormalities |
| 15:00 | Adjust | Real-time reallocation, advance weekly tasks |
| 17:45 | Review | Compile daily reports from four sites |

**Fixed script for roll call / phone round** (answered in 30 seconds, avoiding useless "all fine" reporting):

1. Any new dogs turning yellow or red today? Which ones, and what's the situation?
2. How is yesterday's red list now — improving, holding steady, or worsening?
3. Were all planned tasks (cleaning / feeding / exercise) completed on time? Reasons for any not completed?
4. Is staffing sufficient? Any need for cross-site support?
5. Any supply / equipment issues (cooling, water, disinfectant stock)?

### Handover Key Points (day ↔ night, between shifts)

- **Written record is authoritative, verbal is supplementary**: yellow-and-red list, medication administration, and unfinished tasks are all written into the handover sheet, not left to memory
- **Hand off the red list dog by dog**: for each red dog, confirm in person its current status, next step, and recheck time
- **Log open items**: whatever the previous shift did not finish, clearly write who takes it over and when
- **Supply and equipment status**: cooling, monitoring, disinfectant stock, and abnormal alarms are handed off together

### Tiered Escalation Thresholds

Green → no escalation needed; Yellow → record + hand off and inform the team leader; Red / acute case → phone the manager immediately, and for puppy emergencies / bloat / heavy bleeding / seizures also contact the emergency vet.

### Remote Monitoring and Efficiency Metrics

Cameras patrol the four sites + temperature alarms for the puppy / isolation areas; metrics: capacity-to-care ratio, on-time task completion rate, health closure time, exercise compliance rate, puppy survival rate.

**Core metric definitions and targets** (unified definitions for easy horizontal comparison across the four sites):

| Metric | Definition | Target |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Capacity-to-care ratio | Work-hours required by dogs in residence ÷ actual available work-hours; >1 means overloaded | ≤1 (no long-term overload) |
| On-time task completion rate | Cleaning / feeding / exercise tasks completed on time ÷ planned tasks | ≥95% |
| Health closure time | Time from detecting an abnormality (turning yellow / red) to a resolved outcome | Yellow ≤24h, red same day |
| Exercise compliance rate | Dogs meeting exercise targets ÷ dogs in residence | ≥95% |
| Puppy survival rate | Puppies surviving to weaning ÷ number born | Track monthly, keep improving |

> Managers look at the trend line once a week rather than a single day's value: a continuous decline in any metric at a site is usually an early signal of a staffing, process, or health problem.

## 4. Four Levers for Peak Efficiency

1. **Schedule around the dogs' routine** — clean while dogs are out of their kennels, which neither disturbs the dogs nor wastes labor
2. **Manage by exception** — the manager watches only yellows and reds, not micromanaging 160 dogs
3. **Cross-site flexibility** — pair sites into flexible teams of two
4. **Prevention over remedy** — admission screening, vaccination, disinfection, and weight monitoring head off problems in advance
