## Market Rationale

- **49% of Australian households own a dog** (up from just 40% in 2019); the pet-services market is growing at **10.7% per year**, and boarding accounts for roughly **39.5%** of pet services (the largest single segment)
- In WA, **55%+ of pet-owning households own a dog**, above the national average; Perth is a major boarding market
- **Humanization / premiumization**: one in three owners are willing to spend more on their pet than on some human relatives — fueling demand for boutique boarding, enrichment daycare, and transparency
- **⚠️ The biggest threat**: in-home pet-sitting apps (Mad Paws, acquired by Rover in 2025; Pawshake) competing on low price and convenience, growing at **16–18%**
- **Risks**: labor shortages in the animal-care sector, cost-of-living pressure, tightening regulation (a kennel licence is required in WA to keep more than 6 dogs), and ehrlichiosis having already reached WA

> ⚠️ A note on how the figures are measured: the market shares and growth rates above are all cited from the original reports (AMA, *Pets in Australia 2025*; Grand View; IBISWorld; RAC WA, etc.) and are used here only for planning reference. **Before any formal decision, return to the original reports and confirm the current figures.** The kennel-licence requirement and the dog-count threshold follow **your local council's current regulations**, which may differ from one council to another.

## Strategic Thesis: Do What the In-Home Apps Cannot

Rather than competing with in-home pet-sitting on price and convenience, build the moat on capabilities they cannot replicate — **safe facilities, structured socialization, medical / senior care, transparency, and compliance**. Every evolution in daily staff work should make these values visible.

### Membership / Subscription Tier Example (reference for Phase Two rollout)

Use tiering to turn "premiumization / humanization" demand into recurring, repeatable revenue and to lock in customers against the apps' price war. **Actual prices must be calculated for the local Perth market; the following is a structural example only:**

| Tier | Aimed at | Core benefits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Basic | Occasional boarding | Standard boarding + one photo per day |
| Member | Regulars / daycare | Priority booking + multiple photos/short videos per day + discounted structured enrichment daycare |
| Premium | High-end / multi-dog | Dedicated camera access + custom enrichment/training + medical / senior-care channel + no queuing |

> The value of a subscription is not only revenue but **predictable footfall** — steadying scheduling and staffing calculations, which in turn feeds back into operational quality.

## Four-Phase Roadmap

| Phase | Business focus | Evolution of staff work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **One (0–6 months): Build the foundation** | Compliance, tick control, vaccination thresholds, launching transparency (daily photos + cameras), gathering reviews | Add a photo-and-send-to-owner at each exercise session; fold tick checks into the morning check |
| **Two (6–18 months): Add daycare + membership** | Structured enrichment daycare, membership/subscriptions, tiered camera access | Add daycare activity blocks to the timeline; add a daycare-with-training role; weekday morning drop-off / evening pick-up peaks |
| **Three (18–36 months): Upgrade to a resort** | Medical boarding, senior-dog care (18% growth, high margin), grooming/training/retail, on-call vet | Specialist roles emerge (medical / senior / grooming / dog training); shift from generalists to specialists |
| **Four (3–5 years): Brand + subscription moat** | Unified brand, subscription base, data-driven operations, expansion as conditions allow | Standard SOPs replicated across sites, digital dashboards, staff-retention mechanisms |

### Gates for Advancing to the Next Phase

Don't upgrade just because the calendar says so — **advance only after the previous phase is running smoothly**, to avoid overstretching staff and cash flow:

| From → To | Prerequisites to be met |
| --- | --- |
| One → Two | Compliance in place across all four sites, daily photos an established habit, reviews steadily improving, census not chronically over capacity |
| Two → Three | Daycare/membership proven at the pilot sites (K3/K4) with positive cash flow, daycare-with-training role in place |
| Three → Four | Specialist roles (medical / senior / grooming / dog training) running stably, on-call vet established, single-site SOP taking shape |
| Four → Expansion | SOPs replicable at new sites, digital dashboards able to keep control, retention mechanisms working (falling turnover) |

> If a gate isn't met, **stay put and refine**. Better to be a phase slow than to scale up with holes still in place.

## The Overall Evolution of Staff Work Hours

The **skeleton** of the timeline (two heavy work blocks built around the dogs' routine + on-duty coverage) never changes; the evolution is about **layering higher-value work onto that skeleton**:

Photo transparency → daycare activities → specialist services (medical / senior / grooming / training) → standardization + digitization replicated across sites. **It is not tearing everything down and starting over — it is incremental upgrading.**

## Staff Actions Against the Biggest Threat (In-Home Apps)

1. **Transparency**: send photos/videos to owners every day
2. **Structured socialization**: group play, enrichment — something a single sitter cannot do
3. **Safety and expertise**: safe facilities, vaccination thresholds, biosecurity, medication administration
4. **Consistency**: standard SOPs make the experience consistent across all four sites

## Key Risks and Mitigations

Match every risk flagged in "Market Rationale" to a concrete action — don't just identify risks without responding to them:

| Risk | Mitigating action |
| --- | --- |
| In-home apps taking customers (16–18% growth) | Double down on what apps cannot do: safe facilities, structured socialization, medical care, transparency, consistent SOPs |
| Labor shortage in the animal-care sector | Benchmarked pay + stable scheduling + promotion paths + mentorship (see the retention chapter in the operations plan) |
| Cost-of-living pressure (customer side) | Lock in customers with membership/subscriptions, hold the high end with tiered pricing, sustain the premium with transparency |
| Tightening regulation (kennel licence, etc.) | Proactive compliance, record-keeping, and updating with the council's current regulations as they change |
| Ehrlichiosis having reached WA | Fold tick checks into the morning check, tick screening on intake, environmental tick control, isolate and seek treatment on detection |

> The growth rates and market figures in the risk column follow the same measurement basis cited above and are for planning reference only; defer to the original reports and your local council.

## Implementation Priorities

1. Phase One, do immediately: confirm the kennel licence + tick control + daily photos + gathering reviews
2. Phase Two pilot: trial structured daycare + membership at K3/K4 first, prove it out, then replicate
3. Phase Three preparation: negotiate on-call arrangements with a vet, develop medical / senior-care specialists
4. Throughout: three-color digital dashboard for control + staff training and retention mechanisms
