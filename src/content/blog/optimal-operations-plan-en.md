This plan is not based on guesswork but on veterinary and animal-welfare authorities (ASV Guidelines for Standards of Care in Animal Shelters, AVMA, RSPCA, NACA staffing formulas, Australian state boarding codes of practice, Koret/UF Shelter Medicine), adapted to FluffyWA's reality (4 sites, ~160 adult dogs + 15 puppies, tight staffing). The aim is the best balance of **compliance, biosecurity, welfare and sustainable staffing**.

> Compliance note: Australian boarding rules differ by state (NSW Code of Practice No 5, Victoria's 2025 boarding code, ACT code, etc.). This plan gives evidence-based operating standards; **hard numbers such as minimum enclosure sizes must follow the code of the state each site sits in** — verify against the official documents.

## 1. Intake Gate: Keep Disease Out (highest leverage)

The biggest hidden risk is **pre-clinical shedding** — parvo and kennel cough shed 2–3 days before symptoms appear, so a healthy-looking boarder can be the source. Intake screening is the best-value line of defence.

- **Mandatory C5 vaccination** (not C3): C3 has no kennel-cough protection. C5 = distemper, hepatitis/adenovirus, parvo + Bordetella + parainfluenza
- **Vaccinate at least 14 days ahead**: immunity takes time; injectable Bordetella isn't protective until 5–7 days after the second dose, intranasal 48–72 hours
- **Intake health check**: temperature, demeanour, any cough/diarrhoea; refuse any suspected infectious disease
- **Register**: owner and vet contacts, vaccination proof, diet and medication, behaviour/health history, emergency authorisation

## 2. Isolation Periods: by Pathogen (use the upper bound)

| Situation | Isolation | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Parvo exposure / new-intake observation | **≥14 days** (max incubation 14 days) | UF/dvm360 shelter medicine |
| Kennel cough (CIRDC), symptomatic | **10–14 days** | Worms & Germs / UF |
| Canine influenza H3N2 | **≥21 days** (sheds 3–4 weeks) | ASPCApro / AVMA |
| Vomiting, diarrhoea or coughing | Isolate immediately, dedicated tools, cleaned last | ASV |

Principle: where sources disagree, take the **longer period** as the operating standard.

## 3. Biosecurity Zoning: Healthy First, Sick Last

- **Cleaning order: susceptible→resistant, clean→dirty**. Puppies (K4) first, then healthy adults, **isolation/sick dogs last**
- **Draw a clean/dirty line**: signage on isolation, observation and clean areas — "no animal movement", "authorised staff only"
- **Dedicated, colour-coded tools**: each site, the puppy area and isolation get their own colour-marked brooms, mops and buckets — cleaning tools are themselves fomites, never shared across zones
- **Hand hygiene > footbaths**: wash hands or change gloves between dog groups; **footbaths are largely ineffective** (organic matter disables them) — what works is **zone-dedicated footwear/covers**
- **Solid partitions beat mesh**: better at blocking droplet/aerosol spread, and they cut noise

## 4. Disinfection: Right Agent, Enough Time (parvo is the baseline)

Many "kennel disinfectants" are **QUATs**, and QUATs **do not kill parvovirus** (a non-enveloped virus with a very stable capsid). The plan:

| Disinfectant | Parvo dilution | Contact time | Role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Accelerated hydrogen peroxide (AHP)** | 1:32 (parvo) | **10 min** (general 5 min) | **Daily workhorse**; tolerates light organic load, gentle, first choice for puppy area |
| **Potassium peroxymonosulfate** (Virkon/Trifectant) | per label | **~10 min** | Good with organic load; outbreaks / deep cleaning |
| **Bleach** (sodium hypochlorite) | **1:32** (~30 ml/litre) | **10 min** | Parvo terminal cleaning; mix fresh, rinse and ventilate after |
| ~~QUAT alone~~ | — | — | **Banned as a parvo disinfectant** |

Iron rule: **clean off organic matter first, then disinfect**; it only counts once contact time is met; rinse off bleach etc. afterwards to avoid burning paws.

## 5. Ventilation: the Overlooked Respiratory Defence

- **Target ≥15 air changes per hour (ACH)** (AVMA/ASV suggest 10–20), and it must be **fresh outside air**, not just recirculation
- Poor ventilation lets Bordetella, parainfluenza, adenovirus and canine flu spread by droplets in high-density runs — the breeding ground for a kennel-cough outbreak

## 6. Feeding: Two Meals for Adults, More for Puppies

- **Adults twice daily** (regulatory floor is once; welfare best practice is twice): 9:00 and 15:15
- **Puppies by age**: under 4 months **3–4 meals/day**, 4–6 months twice — the biggest labour driver in the puppy area (K4)
- **Keep the owner's existing diet and feeding times** to reduce GI upset (the most common post-boarding complaint)
- Log each dog's intake within 15 minutes of feeding

## 7. Exercise & Enrichment: Quality Over Duration

- **At least 30 minutes out-of-kennel exercise daily**, or **two sessions of ≥20 minutes** (RSPCA standard, above the regulatory floor)
- Exercise should include **scent/tracking, basic commands, controlled dog socialisation** — not just circuit walking; under-exercised dogs bark more, show more stereotypies and see the vet more often
- **Rotate enrichment daily**: chew toys, puzzle feeders, snuffle mats; without rotation they habituate and stop working
- **Music reduces noise but habituates**: classical/soft rock/reggae lower stress, but the **benefit fades within 2 days to a week — you must change the genre**
- **Group housing needs compatibility screening first**: compatible pairs + enrichment beat single housing for stress, but forcing incompatible dogs together backfires

## 8. Staffing Model: Use "Minutes per Dog", Not Guesswork

The authoritative formula (NACA) is actionable:

- **~9 min/dog/day cleaning + ~6 min/dog/day feeding ≈ 15 min/dog/day** baseline husbandry (excludes exercise, enrichment, admin)
- Company-wide baseline: 160 dogs × 15 min ≈ **40 hours/day**, plus puppies and exercise
- **Ratios vary by activity** — don't apply one number all day:
  - Routine husbandry: about **1 staff : 25–30 dogs**
  - **Supervised exercise/group play: 1 staff : 10–15 dogs** (10 for new/mixed groups)
  - Puppies, medical, behavioural cases: down to 1:8–10
- Method: compute baseline hours from minutes-per-dog, then **check peak periods against the play-supervision ratio**

> This confirms the logic of our current roster: heavy work concentrated in the 1.5-staff core blocks, most staff on hand during exercise, dedicated carer for the puppy area (K4).

## 9. Daily Rhythm: Predictability = the Best Stress Reducer

1. **Health rounds first**: do the day's first round **before** cleaning and feeding — stool, vomit and uneaten food are the earliest signals, and cleaning erases them
2. **Clean while dogs are out**: empty the run during exercise, which cuts fear of cleaning noise/equipment and allows thorough disinfection
3. **Fixed rhythm**: same feeding, exercise and rest times; low-stress handling; read early stress signals (lip-lick, yawn, body tension)
4. **Build in midday rest and quiet zones**: dogs need daytime rest; avoid all-day high arousal
5. **At least 3 health observations daily** (RSPCA standard), recorded on the monitoring sheet

**Suggested day rhythm**: morning health rounds → breakfast → exercise (clean empty runs) → midday rest → enrichment/second walk → dinner → evening settle (music enrichment) → final check.

## 10. Records: Log Every Dog Every Day

Food intake/water, urination and defecation (presence and consistency), demeanour/behaviour change, medications (time, dose, who gave it), exercise and enrichment, any abnormality (lameness, inability to stand, bleeding/swelling, difficulty toileting — must be reported). The value is in the **trend**: one poor-appetite day may just be heat, three in a row is a problem.

## 11. Retain Staff: No Effective Plan Works Without People

Animal-care roles carry high compassion-fatigue and burnout risk, and **understaffing and unstable rosters are the main drivers of turnover**. Evidence-based retention levers (by importance):

1. **Fair, transparent, market-matched pay** (published bands, paid under the Animal Care Award)
2. **Manage workload**: census must not exceed available staff-hours (Capacity for Care) — don't chronically stretch the team
3. **Predictable, stable rosters** (the weekly roster we built) — fixed shift patterns, fair weekend rotation, adequate rest between heavy shifts
4. **Recognition** of hard work; **decompression time** after difficult events
5. **Career path + paid training**: floater → site keeper → site lead; mentorship pairing new with experienced staff
6. **Culture**: respect, team connection, train everyone to spot burnout in each other
