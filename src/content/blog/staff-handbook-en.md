Welcome to FluffyWA! This handbook is every staff member's working guide — from company overview to daily duties, from animal welfare to emergencies. Please read it during onboarding and refer back to it any time. Our mission is simple: **to give every dog a safe, clean and warm home.**

> This handbook is the overview; each specialist area (roster, cleaning, puppy care, breeding, night shift, construction) has its own detailed document, noted in the text. All clinical/biosecurity/regulatory details defer to the supervising vet and the current law of the relevant state.

## Chapter 2 — Roles & Shifts

**Three shift types:**

| Shift | Hours | Duties |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Full shift (site keeper) | 09:00–16:00 (lunch 12–13) | All heavy work |
| Morning floater (duty) | 07:00–13:00 | Morning duty + AM support |
| Evening floater (duty) | 13:00–18:00 | PM support + evening duty |
| Night shift (K4 on-site) | 18:00–07:00 | Overnight puppy care + patrol |

**Progression path**: floater → site keeper → site lead.

**Rostering**: the roster is published a week ahead; shift swaps or cross-site support need ≥2 days' notice; weekends are reception peak with all core staff on. See the *Kennel Work Schedule* and *Night Shift Operations Plan*.

**Shift handover points**: during the full shift's 12:00–13:00 lunch, the morning floater takes over duty so there's no gap; the morning floater opens up at 07:00 and the evening floater covers duty until the 18:00 close — floaters handle opening and closing at both ends, and handovers must be confirmed in person or by phone. Any unfinished items across shifts go into the day's work log, never passed on by word of mouth.

> **New starters trial-shift alongside an experienced teammate before working solo**: before manning duty independently, complete at least one full shift, having personally done opening, feeding, run-cleaning, dog-walking, disinfection and records end-to-end, and confirm you know who to call for an emergency at that site.

## Chapter 3 — Daily Duties

**Core rhythm**: health rounds → feeding → cleaning (while dogs are out) → exercise → disinfection → records → handover.

- **Health rounds first**: do the day's first round before cleaning and feeding — stool, vomit and uneaten food are the earliest health signals
- **Feeding**: strictly per each dog's feeding chart; adults twice daily, puppies 3–4 meals by age; log intake within 15 minutes
- **Cleaning**: empty the run during exercise before cleaning (less fear for the dog, better disinfection)
- **Exercise**: at least two rounds per dog daily, 15–30 min each; large and small dogs separate
- **Records**: log food, elimination, demeanour, medication, exercise and abnormalities per dog per day

Heavy work is concentrated in the high-staff blocks (9–12, 13–16); duty windows are patrol and water only. See the *Daily Company Schedule*.

**Dog-walking and turnout details**:

- Before letting dogs out, put leads on and check the exercise-yard gate is closed, then release them; one person handles the dogs, one cleans the yard, so dogs are never left unattended
- Stagger large dogs, puppies and unfamiliar dogs that don't know each other into separate batches; don't put dogs that haven't been introduced in the same yard; separate immediately if you see chasing/biting, mounting or food-guarding
- On hot days (Perth summer) move exercise to the cooler early morning and evening, keep water in the yard at all times, and stop and cool a dog down immediately if it pants excessively

**Common feeding mistakes**: match each meal to the right dog — check the feeding chart against the dog tag/run, never from memory; for special diets (prescription food, allergies, senior, post-op) check first whether the owner left instructions; collect food bowls and log intake within 15 minutes of finishing, recording leftovers, food-snatching and refusal to eat.

## Chapter 4 — Animal Welfare Standards

This is the core of our work, not an optional extra.

- **The Five Freedoms**: freedom from hunger/thirst, discomfort, pain/injury/disease, freedom to express normal behaviour, freedom from fear/distress
- **Feeding**: fixed time, amount and diet; keep the owner's diet to reduce GI upset
- **Exercise & enrichment**: daily out-of-kennel exercise with scent and social time; rotate enrichment daily; rotate music genres to avoid habituation
- **Low-stress handling**: read early stress signals (lip-lick, yawn, body tension); consistent carers; predictability is the best stress reducer
- **Puppies**: the 15 puppies stay at K4 with a dedicated carer — warmth, small frequent meals, socialisation; unvaccinated puppies don't contact visiting dogs
- See *Effective Dog Care* and the *Puppy Survival Plan*

## Chapter 5 — Cleaning & Biosecurity

**Iron rules**: clean before disinfecting → meet contact time → rinse and dry; never mix chemicals; zone-dedicated tools; cleaning order **puppies first, sick dogs last**.

**Disinfectant essentials**:

- QUATs **do not kill parvovirus** — fine for routine use, but for parvo use bleach 1:32 (10 min contact), accelerated hydrogen peroxide or potassium peroxymonosulfate
- **In the puppy area**, avoid irritating bleach/phenol/QUAT fumes: use gentle chlorhexidine routinely, accelerated hydrogen peroxide for parvo

**Intake biosecurity**: mandatory C5 vaccination, 14 days ahead, health screening, refuse suspected infectious disease.

See the *Cleaning & Disinfection Schedule*, *Adult vs Puppy Cleaning SOP* and *Optimal Operations Plan*.

## Chapter 6 — Health & Safety (Staff)

- **Chemical safety**: store separately, keep original labels, post dilutions on the wall; wear PPE (gloves, plus goggles/mask as needed); **bleach + ammonia cleaner = toxic gas, never mix**
- **Personal safety**: leads on when walking dogs; close gates behind you; stay alert around large and unfamiliar dogs; wash and report any bite/scratch immediately
- **Hand hygiene**: wash hands or change gloves between dog groups — the cheapest, most effective defence
- **Physical protection**: correct lifting technique; in hot weather (Perth summer) keep yourself hydrated and avoid heat stress
- **Night safety**: lone night staff check in with the duty manager every 2 hours

## Chapter 7 — Emergency Procedures

**Escalation chain**: spot an issue → notify the site lead/duty manager immediately → call the vet if needed. **The emergency contact list is posted in every duty room.**

- **Adult emergencies**: isolate and report poor appetite, diarrhoea or coughing; bloat/GDV in large deep-chested dogs is a time-critical emergency; seizures, major bleeding, respiratory distress → call the emergency vet immediately
- **Puppy emergencies**: not nursing, cold and limp, seizing, vomiting/diarrhoea — puppies deteriorate within hours, **when in doubt call**, warm them and prevent hypoglycaemia first, then transport (see the *Puppy Survival Plan*)
- **Fire/safety**: know the evacuation plan, extinguisher locations and dog evacuation routes
- **Escape**: notify the lead at once, seal exits, coordinate recovery; don't chase a panicked dog (the more you chase, the more it runs) — guide it with treats, a familiar carer and a calm voice, and note the time and direction it went missing

**Before you pick up the phone to report, get three things clear** (you don't need all three, but the more complete the faster): which site and which run, which dog (tag/name), and what symptoms you see and for how long. For emergencies the principle is phone first, treat second — when in doubt call 0405 098 765; better to report once too often than to delay.

| Situation | First step | Who follows up |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Minor abnormality (single-day leftover food, soft stool) | Record + isolate and observe | Site lead reviews the trend next day |
| Dog emergency (bloat, seizures, major bleeding, respiratory distress) | Call immediately + contact the emergency vet | Lead/duty manager attends |
| Puppy in doubt (not nursing, cold and limp) | Warm first to prevent hypoglycaemia, call immediately | Duty manager + vet |
| Staff bite/scratch | Rinse and disinfect immediately, stop bleeding | Report to lead, seek medical care if needed and record |
| Escape/fire/intrusion | Act on the plan + report by phone | Duty manager coordinates |

## Chapter 8 — Records & Handover

- **Full daily record per dog**: intake, water, urination/defecation, demeanour/behaviour, medication, exercise, abnormalities — the value is in the **trend** (one poor-appetite day may be heat; three in a row is a problem)
- **Drop-off/pick-up**: handled face-to-face at K4 reception hours, signed
- **Cross-site transfers**: both site leads confirm health and sign off
- **Handover**: confirm face-to-face, miss nothing; night handover by phone or group chat

## Chapter 9 — Code of Conduct

- **Welfare first**: never handle a dog roughly; report any abuse you witness
- **Reliable attendance**: on time (early shift opens at 7:00); request leave/swaps ahead
- **Teamwork**: support other sites; respect colleagues; communicate proactively
- **Customer service**: courteous and professional; report the dog's status honestly; protect customer privacy
- **Confidentiality**: don't disclose customer or company information
- **Integrity**: record honestly, never hide abnormalities; never conceal safety or health issues

## Chapter 10 — Pay & Benefits

- Paid under the **Animal Care and Veterinary Services Award**, casual loading included, night/weekend penalty rates paid correctly
- **Stable rosters**: published a week ahead, fixed patterns, fair weekend rotation
- **Progression & training**: clear path + paid training; new staff paired with an experienced mentor
- **Support**: decompression time after difficult events; we watch for compassion fatigue and burnout and look out for each other
