A good kennel's disease control, welfare, and operational efficiency are determined from the architectural design onward. This guide is compiled from Australian state codes (Victoria Boarding Code, NSW Code No. 5, ACT/WA/QLD) and international facility standards (ASV Guidelines, AVMA, Koret Shelter Medicine, dvm360 kennel design), and is oriented toward a multi-site operation of roughly 160 dogs plus a puppy nursery.

> **Two important notes:** (1) Australian boarding construction regulations **vary by state**, and **council (local government) planning approvals (noise, boundary setbacks, acoustic fencing) are often stricter than state codes** — each site must be checked individually against its local council's requirements. (2) Some precise figures in this guide are drawn from authoritative sources (official PDFs cannot be accessed directly in this environment); **where "verify against code" is flagged, refer to the official source text before formal construction.**

## 1. Enclosure Dimensions

**Victoria Boarding Code** (mandatory under the *Domestic Animals Act*) [verify against code]:

- Minimum enclosure area for a single adult dog (>16 weeks) (reported figures, verify): small breeds approx. 1.5 m², medium approx. 2.4 m², large approx. 3.0 m²
- **1/3 of each enclosure must be weatherproof, with a raised sleeping area**
- **Key exercise-area rule:** if each enclosure meets "minimum area + 20 m² per dog," then no separate exercise area is required (exercise can occur within the enclosure); for dogs in enclosures >20 m², no additional exercise is required unless boarded for more than 4 weeks; dogs housed in a minimum-size enclosure for more than 2 weeks must be exercised daily

**NSW Code No. 5:** performance-based, with few hard figures — each dog must be able to eat, sleep, sit and lie down, stretch its limbs, stretch out, and move about, and the enclosure height must allow for cleaning.

**ACT:** adjacent enclosures must be separated by **solid, opaque partitions** (for both disease control and noise reduction).

## 2. Floors and Surfaces

- **Materials:** interior surfaces must be **impervious, durable, and washable**; **sealed concrete is ideal** (unsealed, porous concrete traps moisture, pathogens, and odours, and must be epoxy-sealed)
- **Slope:** floors should slope toward the drain at **1–2%** (approx. 1/4 inch per foot) to prevent pooling
- **Coving:** round the 90° floor-to-wall junction to prevent water from collecting in the seam and to ease cleaning and disease control
- **Preventing cross-enclosure contamination:** poorly poured floors leave gaps under partitions that let effluent flow between adjacent enclosures; use a two-part floor seal + a flexible mat under the partition so that liquid does not migrate even if the top seal is damaged

## 3. Drainage and Waste

- **One drain channel per row of back-to-back enclosures**, with floors sloping toward it at approx. 2%; fine-mesh basket screens can be fitted in the channel to catch hair and waste
- **No shared drainage:** each enclosure/pen must not drain through a neighbour; effluent must not cross compartments (disease control). Solid biosecurity walls keep wash water contained within each compartment
- **Drain channel slope at least 0.5%**, one-directional; **avoid bends and intersections** ("waste doesn't like to turn corners"), and break long channels into sections to prevent excessive depth
- **Cleaning frequency** (ACT/Victoria, operational level, verify): faeces cleared at least once daily; enclosures hosed at least once daily; enclosures/pens disinfected at least 3 times per week

## 4. Ventilation and Air Quality

- **Target 10–20 air changes per hour (ACH)** (AVMA companion animal standard; dog areas in practice are often 8–12, cat areas 10–20)
- **Fresh air first:** the best approach is **100% outdoor fresh air, no recirculation**, to dilute bioaerosols; **building-wide shared recirculating air conditioning spreads disease between enclosures** — use energy recovery ventilation (ERV) to make 100% fresh air more cost-effective
- **Independent air supply to each enclosure/compartment**, to prevent air from migrating from one enclosure to another
- **Humidity controlled at approx. 30–70%**; the NSW code requires ventilation to keep the space free of dampness, foul odour, and draughts

## 5. Temperature Control (Especially Critical for Perth's Heat)

- **Victoria code** [verify against code]: within fully enclosed, mechanically ventilated buildings, temperature must be maintained at **15°C – 27°C**
- Common Australian kennel guidance: no lower than 10°C, no higher than 26°C (secondary source, verify); AVMA: above 15.5°C, below 26.6°C
- **NSW:** performance-based — protect dogs from temperature extremes; young/old dogs may need heating or cooling
- **Perth practicalities:** WA currently has only the *Standards and Guidelines for Dog Health and Welfare* (2020) + the *Animal Welfare Act 2002*, with dedicated regulations still in draft; given Perth's summer heat, sufficient cooling capacity must be built to hold the ≤26–27°C upper limit, with shade and protection against extreme weather

## 6. Walls, Partitions, and Noise

- **Solid partitions beat wire mesh:** solid partitions block droplet/splash disease transmission and eliminate visual stimuli (reducing chain barking); wire mesh does neither. ACT mandates solid, opaque partitions between adjacent enclosures
- **Biosecurity wall materials:** solid, non-porous, impervious (e.g. sealed cement), and **able to resist scratching and biting**, keeping effluent contained within each compartment. Solid partitions should reach at least standing-dog height, opening only at the front where a line of sight is needed
- **Noise:** kennels are frequently measured at **70–100+ dBA** (the ideal target approaches the residential level of 45 dBA). **Install sound-absorbing materials on upper walls and ceilings** (foam/fibreglass panels, PVC-faced or perforated hardboard) to absorb high-frequency barking and reduce reverberation; **line-of-sight barriers** reduce chain barking
- **Height/escape prevention:** partitions must be tall enough and non-climbable to prevent escape

## 7. Fencing and Security

- **Victoria code:** boundary/perimeter fencing **minimum height 1.8 m** [verify against code]
- Construction (reported, verify): solid materials such as brick, concrete, timber, or iron; or chain-link of 3.15 mm wire diameter with 50 mm mesh; or 4 mm welded mesh with mesh ≤50 mm
- **Security barrier** (ACT/general): must prevent escape and unauthorised entry; at least one self-closing, lockable gate
- **Double-gate/airlock entry:** prevents dogs bolting when one gate is open
- **Dig prevention:** for dogs that like to dig, run a concrete footing along the fence or bury mesh below ground (Dogs Queensland guidelines)

## 8. Functional Zoning

A well-designed facility requires the following **physically separated** areas:

- **Reception/check-in area**
- **Isolation/quarantine area (most critical):** independent entrance, **independent ventilation, independent drainage, independent cleaning tools**; positioned so that dogs enter and exit without passing healthy dogs; respiratory-disease dogs spaced **≥ approx. 6 m / several empty enclosures apart**
- **Puppy nursery/whelping room:** separate, warm, low-traffic, high-biosecurity
- **Grooming area:** ideally split into three spaces for bathing, drying, and grooming
- **Food preparation + storage** (kitchen, refrigeration, dry-goods storage)
- **Laundry** (high throughput: washers, dryers, folding)
- **Storage room**
- **Exercise yard** (separate from housing; see the Victoria +20 m²/dog rule)
- **Staff area/office/records**

## 9. Utilities and Services

- **Water supply:** continuous clean water supply; **hot water is essential for cleaning and disinfection**; hand-wash sinks, drains, and hose connections distributed throughout the housing area
- **Lighting:** provide a regular day/night light cycle (natural or artificial), with even, diffuse light sufficient for cleaning and inspecting dogs; **prioritise natural light** (windows/skylights), and use **flicker-free LED** for artificial lighting (dogs can perceive fluorescent flicker and hum)
- **Power/backup:** cooling and heating (holding 15–27°C and the puppy nursery), ventilation fans, and hot water are all life-safety critical → provide **backup power/redundancy**, especially for the puppy nursery and isolation-area HVAC under Perth's heat

## 10. Puppy Nursery Temperatures (Consistent with the *Improving Puppy Survival Plan*)

- First approx. 4 days: ambient **29.5–32°C**, approx. 30°C inside the whelping box/nest
- Days 7–10: reduce to approx. 26.7°C
- End of week 4: approx. 22.2°C
- Puppies cannot self-regulate temperature until about 2.5 weeks: provide a **heating pad + warm environment + one cool retreat zone** so puppies can self-regulate; watch for huddling behaviour [Victoria also has a *Code of Practice for the Operation of Breeding and Rearing Businesses*; mandatory puppy-nursery specifications defer to that code, verify]

## Construction Priorities and Key Reminders

**Scale determines the constraints:** at the scale of multiple sites + roughly 160 dogs + a nursery, the real hard constraints are usually the combination of **council planning approvals (noise attenuation, boundary setbacks, acoustic fencing), state boarding codes, and building regulations** — welfare codes provide the **minimum standard**, while council conditions are often higher (acoustic reports, setbacks). **Each site (especially the Perth locations) must be checked individually against its local council's requirements.**

**The three construction investments with the greatest disease-control impact:** (1) sealed, waterproof floors + independent drainage (no cross-enclosure flow); (2) 100% fresh air, with independent air supply to each enclosure; (3) an isolation area with independent entrance/ventilation/drainage. Getting these three right at the construction stage makes disease control far easier later.

> This guide is designed to accompany the *Kennel Work Schedule*, *Cleaning and Disinfection Schedule*, *Most Effective Operations Plan*, and *Improving Puppy Survival Plan*. All hard figures for dimensions, temperatures, fencing, and the like defer to the current state legislation and council conditions applicable to your site.
