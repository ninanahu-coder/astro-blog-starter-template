This document is the standard operating procedure (SOP) for cleaning at the FluffyWA kennel. It sets out cleaning rules for **adult dogs** and **puppies** separately, because the risks and methods for the two are entirely different: the adult area focuses on maintaining hygiene and disease prevention, while the puppy area focuses on maximum disease control and gentle chemical use. All staff must read this document and follow it before starting work.

> A single principle runs through the entire manual: **clean first (remove organic matter), then disinfect (kill pathogens), allow enough contact time, then rinse and dry.** Clinical and disease-prevention thresholds are subject to the supervising veterinarian's judgment.

## Universal Ground Rules (apply to both adults and puppies)

1. **Order: clean first, then disinfect**—residual organic matter deactivates quats and bleach, so the order must never be reversed
2. **Contact time is a hard requirement**—rinsing immediately after spraying = no disinfection; use a timer
3. **Cleaning direction: susceptible → resistant, clean → dirty**—puppies first, healthy adults next, isolation/sick dogs last
4. **Zoned, color-coded equipment**—the adult area, puppy area, and isolation area each use dedicated equipment; borrowing across zones is strictly prohibited (the equipment itself is a transmission vehicle)
5. **Never mix chemicals**—bleach + ammonia-based cleaner = toxic gas; bleach + quats deactivate each other
6. **Hand hygiene**—wash hands or change gloves between contact with different groups of dogs; change shoes or use dedicated shoes when switching groups
7. **Clean while dogs are out**—empty the kennel run during exercise time before cleaning, to reduce dogs' fear of noise and equipment and to allow thorough disinfection

# Part One: Adult Dog Cleaning

## Adult Cleaning Frequency Table

| Item | Frequency |
| --- | --- |
| Immediate removal of kennel feces/urine | Anytime, clean as soon as spotted |
| Kennel floor cleaning | Daily |
| Food and water bowl washing | Daily (food bowls after each meal) |
| Bedding change | Daily or whenever soiled |
| Kennel disinfection | Once daily |
| Exercise yard clearing | Daily |
| Exercise yard deep cleaning | Weekly |
| Kennel deep disinfection | At least once weekly; before a new dog moves in; after an outbreak |
| Turnover (a different dog moves into the same run) | Thorough cleaning + disinfection each time |

## Adult Daily Cleaning Steps

1. **Move the dog out** to the exercise yard or a temporary kennel (do not spray chemicals while the dog is present)
2. **Dry cleaning**: scoop feces, remove leftover food, clear soiled bedding, sweep up hair (dry before wet, to reduce pathogen spread)
3. **Detergent scrubbing**: floor, wall bases, cage doors, with focus on corners and drains
4. **Rinse clean** to remove detergent residue
5. **Apply disinfectant**: for routine use, accelerated hydrogen peroxide (5–10 minutes) or a quat (10 minutes); spray evenly and **allow the full contact time**
6. **Rinse + dry**: after using bleach and similar disinfectants, always rinse to avoid burning the dogs' paws; dampness breeds bacteria and mold, so drying is essential
7. **Food and water bowls**: wash daily, soak in disinfectant solution, rinse and dry
8. **Bedding**: change it, and wash and tumble-dry soiled bedding at high temperature

## Adult Disinfectant Selection

| Disinfectant | Use | Contact Time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Accelerated hydrogen peroxide (AHP) | **Daily workhorse**, gentle and easy to rinse | 5–10 minutes |
| Quaternary ammonium (QUAT) | Routine daily use (**does not kill parvo**) | About 10 minutes |
| Potassium peroxymonosulfate | Weekly deep disinfection, disease prevention | About 10 minutes |
| Bleach 1:32 | Terminal disinfection for parvo outbreaks/runs with a history | 10 minutes, rinse and ventilate |

# Part Two: Puppy Cleaning (Strictest)

Puppies (especially those not fully vaccinated) are the most vulnerable group in the kennel: a long window of parvo susceptibility, delicate airways, and sensitivity to disinfectant odors. The puppy area (K4) must be **completely separate** from the adult groups, using dedicated equipment and dedicated PPE, ideally staffed by a designated person.

## Puppy Cleaning Frequency Table

| Item | Frequency |
| --- | --- |
| Immediate removal of waste | Anytime, more often than for adults (puppies eliminate frequently) |
| Nursery/run floor cleaning | Daily, ≥1–2 times |
| Washing of food bowls, water bowls, and feeding utensils | After each feeding (puppies eat 3–4 meals per day) |
| Bedding change | Daily, and whenever soiled/damp (puppies lose heat easily) |
| Routine run cleaning and disinfection | Daily |
| Nursery deep disinfection (during vacant turnover) | Every turnover + weekly |
| Litter change/new puppy move-in | Thorough cleaning + parvo-killing disinfection each time |

## Puppy Cleaning Steps (key differences from adults)

1. **Move puppies to a safe, warm place**—puppies cannot self-regulate temperature before 4 weeks of age; keep them warm during cleaning, as cold and damp can be fatal
2. **Dry-clean** waste and soiled bedding
3. **Scrub with a gentle detergent**, thoroughly removing organic matter
4. **Rinse clean**
5. **Disinfect—this is the biggest difference between puppies and adults** (see below)
6. **Ventilate thoroughly**: after disinfection, always ventilate, rinse, and dry before returning puppies to the run—**never let disinfectant odor accumulate around the puppies**
7. **Keep dry and warm**: put down clean, dry bedding

## Puppy Disinfectant Selection (Critical)

**Around newborn puppies, avoid the volatile odors of bleach, phenolics (pine oil/Lysol type), and quats**—the fumes can harm the airways and trigger fading puppy syndrome.

| Scenario | What to use | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Routine gentle cleaning around newborn puppies | **Chlorhexidine** | Gentle, but **note: chlorhexidine does not kill parvo** |
| When parvo must be killed (vacant-run turnover, post-exposure, new puppy move-in) | **Accelerated hydrogen peroxide 1:16, about 5 minutes** | Relatively gentle, less irritating than bleach; **first choice for killing parvo in the puppy area** |
| Terminal disinfection for a parvo outbreak | Bleach 1:32, 10 minutes | Puppies must be moved out first; after disinfection, ventilate thoroughly, rinse, and dry before returning them |

> Because chlorhexidine does not kill parvo, and bleach odor is harmful to puppies, the puppy area preferentially uses **accelerated hydrogen peroxide** to kill parvo—balancing "kills it" with "gentle enough."

## Additional Precautions for the Puppy Area

- **Completely separate**: dedicated broom, mop, bucket, and cloths, never shared with the adult area
- **Puppies come first in the cleaning order**: over the course of a day, staff do the puppy area first, then adults, and finally isolation/sick dogs—to avoid carrying pathogens to the most vulnerable puppies
- **Change clothing when switching groups**: wash hands before and after entering the puppy area, and ideally change shoes or wear dedicated shoes and change your outer garment
- **Do not disrupt warming and feeding rhythm during cleaning**: hypothermia and hypoglycemia are two of the three big killers of puppies, so prioritize keeping puppies from getting chilled while cleaning

## One-Page Quick Comparison

| | Adults | Puppies |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Floor cleaning | Daily | Daily, 1–2 times |
| Bowl washing | After each meal (2 meals/day) | After each meal (3–4 meals/day) |
| Daily disinfectant | Accelerated hydrogen peroxide / quat | **Chlorhexidine** (gentle) |
| Killing parvo | Bleach 1:32 / AHP / potassium peroxymonosulfate | **Accelerated hydrogen peroxide 1:16** (preferred) |
| Deep disinfection | Weekly + turnover | Weekly + every turnover |
| Cleaning order | After puppies, before isolation | **First** |
| Special attention | Rinse to protect paws, dry | **Avoid irritating odors, keep warm, dedicated equipment** |
