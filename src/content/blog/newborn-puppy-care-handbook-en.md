Newborn puppies are born blind, deaf, unable to regulate their own body temperature and unable to eliminate on their own — their lives depend entirely on the dam and their carers. This handbook is for FluffyWA puppy-area (K4) staff and walks through day-to-day care from birth to 8 weeks, week by week. The figures are drawn from authoritative veterinary sources (**Merck Veterinary Manual, AKC, WSAVA, AAHA, AVSAB, CAPC, Royal Canin Academy, Cornell/UF shelter medicine**). It is the companion to the *Puppy Survival Plan*: that plan explains the "why"; this handbook covers "what to do every day".

> **Important disclaimer**: the temperatures, feeding amounts and weights in this handbook are general references. **Every clinical decision must be confirmed by the supervising vet.** A newborn puppy can deteriorate within hours — when in doubt, call the vet immediately; do not wait and see.

## 1. The First 24 Hours: The Most Critical Day

**Immediately after birth (normally done by the dam — step in only if she doesn't):**

1. Make sure every puppy's **airway is clear**: the dam tears the membrane and licks the puppy dry; if she doesn't, immediately tear the membrane away from the nose and mouth, gently wipe the airways with a clean towel, dry the puppy and rub to stimulate breathing. **Do not "swing" a puppy to clear fluid** (the head-snap carries a brain-haemorrhage risk) — wrap in a towel and rub instead
2. The dam biting through the umbilical cord is fine; if handled manually, tie off and cut about 2–3 cm from the belly and disinfect the stump with iodine (povidone-iodine)
3. Get every puppy onto **colostrum as soon as possible** — colostrum is rich in IgG antibodies; gut absorption **peaks around 4 hours after birth, essentially stops after 12–16 hours, and "gut closure" occurs by ~24 hours** (Merck); **the first 12 hours are a hard window**
4. Check each puppy: mouth and nose (cleft palate), limbs, anus, umbilicus; record sex and coat markings
5. **Weigh each puppy at 12 hours and again at 24 hours** to start its weight chart

**Normal newborn vitals (AKC / Merck — report deviations):**

| Parameter | Week-1 newborn |
| --- | --- |
| Rectal temperature | **35.0–36.7°C** (low by adult standards, but normal for a newborn); rises to 37–38°C by weeks 2–3 |
| Heart rate | **> 200 bpm** |
| Respiration | **about 15–35 breaths/min** |
| Sleep | about **90%** of the day (the rest spent nursing) |

**Look after the dam at the same time**: confirm the number of placentas matches the number of puppies (a retained placenta causes uterine infection), offer water and food, and have the vet examine her within 24–48 hours of whelping.

## 2. Warmth: Hypothermia Is Killer Number One

Puppies **cannot regulate their own temperature until about 4 weeks of age** (Merck). Place a heat mat or heat lamp over **only about half of the whelping box** so the dam and puppies can move away from the heat, and keep the box out of draughts.

**Ambient temperature by age (AKC / Merck):**

| Age | Whelping-box ambient temperature |
| --- | --- |
| Days 1–4 | **29.4–32.2°C** |
| Days 7–10 | step down to **about 26.7°C** |
| End of week 4 | **about 22.2°C** |

![Warmth: heat covers only half the box; read the sleeping posture](/puppy-care/warmth-en.svg)

*Heat covers only about half the box so puppies can move away — the sleeping posture tells you hot from cold at a glance.*

- Healthy puppies sleep quietly in a pile; **spread far apart = too hot; piled tightly, crying, climbing onto the heat source = too cold**
- A **core temperature below 35°C** causes the heart rate to drop, the suckle reflex to vanish and the gut to shut down — hypothermia, hypoglycaemia and dehydration compound each other into the classic **"fading puppy syndrome"**
- **Never feed a chilled puppy**: hypothermia stops the gut, so milk sits and ferments → fatal reflux and aspiration. Rewarm **slowly** over 30–60 minutes first (wrapped against your body, or a warm-water bottle behind a towel; rewarming too fast worsens dehydration), and only feed once the puppy feels warm and has a suckle reflex

## 3. Feeding: Nursing First, Bottle-Feeding by Protocol

**Nursing (the default):**

- For the first 2 weeks puppies spend most of the day nursing and sleeping; confirm **every** puppy gets a teat, and place weaker ones onto the fuller rear teats by hand
- A full puppy settles and sleeps; **constant crying and restless rooting = not getting enough** — check the dam's milk and consider supplementing
- Check the dam's mammary glands daily: redness, hardness, heat, or discoloured milk (mastitis signs) — report to the vet immediately

**Bottle-feeding (orphans, or topping up when milk is short — per Merck / Royal Canin):**

- Use **canine milk replacer only** (e.g. Esbilac), mixed per the label, warmed to about **38°C** before feeding — test on the inside of your wrist; **never cow's milk** (wrong protein/fat/lactose ratio, causes diarrhoea)
- Reference amount: about **30 ml per 100 g body weight per day**, split across feeds; energy needs are roughly **15–25 kcal per 100 g body weight per day** (rising with age) — calibrate against the replacer's label and the weight-gain curve
- Frequency: **every 2–3 hours in week 1 (including overnight)**, every 3–4 hours in week 2, every 4–6 hours from week 3 — matching the 8–10 natural nursings a day, chiefly to prevent hypoglycaemia
- Position: puppy **on its belly, head slightly raised** (mimicking natural nursing); never feed a puppy on its back — milk goes into the airway
- Teat hole size: milk should drip slowly when the bottle is inverted; burp the puppy gently after each feed
- Mixed formula keeps a maximum of 24 hours refrigerated; discard whatever is left in the bottle after a feed

![Feeding position: on the belly with head up is right; feeding on the back causes choking](/puppy-care/feeding-en.svg)

*Feed with the puppy on its belly, head slightly raised (like natural nursing); **never on its back** — milk goes straight into the airway.*

**Hypoglycaemia prevention**: newborns have almost no glycogen reserve — liver glycogen runs out after about 24 hours without food (faster in a sick puppy). Reference blood glucose: normal about **90–200 mg/dL** (the vet's judgement governs). Signs: weakness, tremors, persistent crying, cold body, going limp. Prevention is **frequent feeding + warmth**, not last-minute rescue.

## 4. Elimination Stimulation: No Independent Toileting for 3 Weeks

Puppies **cannot urinate or defecate on their own before about 3 weeks**; the dam's licking triggers it. Dam-reared puppies need no help; **for orphans or puppies the dam ignores, gently wipe the anus and genital area with a warm damp cotton ball or soft cloth before and after every feed** until they eliminate, and log it:

- **Normal urine**: pale yellow, good volume; dark yellow/orange and scanty = dehydration sign
- **Normal stool**: yellow-brown, formed-soft; watery, green, bloody, or no stool for over 24 hours = report to the vet

## 5. Weighing & Developmental Milestones: The Scale Every Day

**Weight is the most sensitive early-warning signal** — it drops before other symptoms show (AKC).

- **Weigh at the same time every day on a gram scale**, log it in each puppy's chart, for at least the first 3 weeks
- Normal gain: **about 5–10% of birth weight per day**; birth weight should **double by day 7–10**
- **Red flag: any weight loss (especially >4%) or 2 consecutive days without gain** — report the same day and find the cause (losing out on teats? dam's milk? box temperature?)

![Weight curve: normal gain is +5–10% per day, doubling by day 7–10; flat or falling is a red flag](/puppy-care/weight-en.svg)

*The green line is normal growth (doubling by day 7–10); a flat or falling red line is a red flag — call the vet the same day.*

**Developmental milestones (AKC / Merck — report significant delays):**

| Week | Milestone |
| --- | --- |
| Weeks 1–2 | Eyes and ears closed, crawling only; about 90% of the day asleep |
| Week 2 | **Eyes open (days 10–14)**, ear canals open shortly after (days 14–18) |
| Week 3 | Standing, first steps, baby teeth erupt; starts eliminating and thermoregulating on its own (still needs support) |
| Week 4 | Walking steadily, playing with littermates, clearly reacting to sounds and people |
| Weeks 5–8 | Weaning completed, vaccines started, prime socialisation window in full swing |

## 6. Whelping Box & Hygiene: Clean, but Don't Over-Disturb

- Change box bedding (towels/pads) **daily**, and immediately whenever soiled; launder used bedding hot per the puppy-area laundry protocol
- Minimise non-essential contact in the first 2 weeks; **wash hands and change/disinfect footwear on the way in and out of the puppy area** — parvo control starts on day one (see the *Puppy Survival Plan*)
- **Disinfectant choice in the nursery (key to parvo control)**: use **chlorhexidine** for gentle everyday cleaning around puppies, but note **chlorhexidine does not kill parvovirus**; when you need to kill parvo (box turnover, after exposure), first clean thoroughly to remove organic matter, then use **bleach at 1:32 with ≥10 minutes contact** or **accelerated hydrogen peroxide**, and **ventilate well, rinse and dry fully** before returning puppies — never let bleach/phenol fumes build up around newborns
- Deep-clean and disinfect the whelping box at least weekly (move dam and puppies out, use disinfectant per the puppy-area cleaning SOP, dry fully before returning them)
- Until vaccinations are complete, puppies have **no contact with outside dogs and do not visit shared dog areas**

## 7. Worming & Vaccination Schedule

The vet's programme always takes precedence; general reference (consistent with the *Puppy Survival Plan*):

| Item | Timing | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Worming (roundworm/hookworm — passed via placenta and milk, treat even without symptoms) | **at 2, 4, 6 and 8 weeks**, then monthly to 6 months | CAPC / AAHA |
| First core vaccine (DHPP: parvo/distemper combo) | **starting 6–8 weeks of age** | AAHA / WSAVA |
| Boosters | every 2–4 weeks, **last dose ≥16 weeks of age** | AAHA / WSAVA 2024 |
| Protection kicks in | only **about 1–2 weeks after the final dose** | — |
| Dam | wormed on the same schedule as the litter (lactation-safe products per the vet) | — |

> **Susceptibility window**: maternal antibodies halve roughly every 10 days, dropping (typically 8–12 weeks, some puppies to 16 weeks) to a level too low to protect yet still able to interfere with vaccines — which is why a **series** of vaccines is needed and why a half-vaccinated puppy is still genuinely at risk.

## 8. Weaning & Early Socialisation

**Weaning (weeks 3–8, gradual — Royal Canin):**

- **Weeks 3–4**: offer puppy food blended to a gruel with warm water (milk replacer can be mixed in) in a shallow dish, 3–4 times daily, free licking; nursing continues
- **Weeks 5–6**: thicken the gruel step by step with less water; puppy food becomes the main meal, nursing the top-up
- **Weeks 7–8**: fully transitioned to soaked/dry puppy food with fresh water always available; **puppies do not leave the dam and litter before 8 weeks**

**Early socialisation (starts from day 3):**

- Weeks 1–2: gently handle every puppy **1–2 times a day for a few minutes** (weighing is the perfect opportunity) so they learn human scent and touch
- From weeks 3–4: introduce everyday sounds (a radio at low volume, footsteps, equipment noise), different bedding textures and safe toys
- Weeks 5–8: positive human interaction daily, exposure to different people (hats, male and female voices), short alone-time practice
- **AVSAB position**: the critical socialisation window is about **weeks 3–14**, and missing it causes lifelong fear/aggression; behaviour problems (not infectious disease) are the leading cause of death in dogs under 3, so socialisation **should not** wait for the full vaccine course — once a puppy has had **one vaccine plus 7 days, and its first worming**, it can have controlled contact with **known healthy/vaccinated dogs on disinfected surfaces**
- Rule: **everything stays voluntary and positive for the puppy**; if one hides or cries, back off and slow down

## 9. Dam Postpartum Care

The dam's condition decides the whole litter's survival. Check and log daily:

- **Appetite and water**: a lactating dam may eat 2–3× her normal amount — free-feed a puppy-food formula and keep fresh water available
- **Discharge (lochia)**: dark red/brown-green, odourless discharge for some weeks post-whelping is normal; **purulent, foul-smelling, or heavy fresh-red bleeding = call the vet immediately** (uterine infection / pyometra)
- **Mammary glands**: palpate daily; red, hard, hot, painful glands or bloody/discoloured milk = mastitis — report to the vet
- **Eclampsia (milk fever / low calcium) red flags**: restlessness, panting, stiff gait, trembling, seizures — **this is an emergency**, most common in **small-breed** dams in weeks 1–4 post-whelping (WSAVA / Merck)
- Dam refusing to care for puppies, frequently leaving the box, or rough with them: report to the supervisor and assess switching to hand-rearing

## 10. Red-Flag Quick Reference: See One, Report One

Any single item below — **report and contact the vet immediately**, do not leave it for the next shift:

![Red-flag quick-reference card: see one, report one; when in doubt, phone the vet now](/puppy-care/redflags-en.svg)

- Weight loss (especially >4%) or repeated failure to gain
- Puppy feels cold, is limp, suckles weakly or won't seek the teat
- Crying that lasts longer than one feed interval
- Diarrhoea (especially bloody/foul), vomiting, bloated belly
- Laboured or open-mouth breathing, milk from the nose (aspiration), pale/blue gums
- Signs of dehydration (tacky gums, slow skin snap-back, scanty dark urine)
- Red, swollen or oozing umbilicus
- Tremors, twitching, seizures (hypoglycaemia/hypothermia)
- A puppy clearly separated from the litter or pushed out of the nest by the dam (the dam often notices a sick puppy first)

## Daily Care Checklist (K4 Nursery)

- [ ] Ambient temperature on target for age (days 1–4: 29–32°C); read puppy sleeping posture for hot/cold
- [ ] Rewarm a chilled puppy 30–60 min before feeding — **never feed a cold puppy**
- [ ] Confirm every puppy got colostrum in the first 12 hours
- [ ] Orphans on canine milk replacer, warmed to 38°C, every 2–3 hours in week 1 (incl. overnight)
- [ ] Stimulate elimination before/after each feed for orphans in the first 3 weeks; log urine/stool
- [ ] **Weigh at the same time daily**; red flag: no gain / loss >4% → call the vet
- [ ] Change bedding daily and when soiled; wash hands and change/disinfect footwear in and out
- [ ] Worm at 2/4/6/8 weeks; vaccinate from 6–8 weeks every 2–4 weeks, last dose ≥16 weeks
- [ ] Start socialisation after one vaccine + 7 days and first worming; only healthy vaccinated dogs
- [ ] Check the dam daily for appetite, lochia, mammary glands; treat eclampsia signs as an emergency
- [ ] Red-flag chart posted on the nursery wall; when in doubt, phone
